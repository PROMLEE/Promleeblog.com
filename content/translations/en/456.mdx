## Before We Begin

An earnings release says AI-related revenue grew. That sounds like business is going well, but the same release shows that investment in servers and data centers grew faster. To see whether higher sales translated into more cash, read the cash flow statement. **Technology news and cash generation tell you different things about a business.**
<br/>
We'll practice reading Microsoft's financial statements. Earlier examples covered a hypothetical document service. Here we look at the whole company without applying a small service's margin to it. This article offers no buy or sell judgment or price target. The sources were checked on October 16, 2026.

## Reporting Period and Disclosure Scope

Microsoft's fiscal 2026 ended June 30, 2026. It filed its 10-K on July 29. The annual figures below are in millions of U.S. dollars. [SEC filing details](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526323660/0001193125-26-323660-index.htm)
The earnings release from the same day contains both quarterly and annual figures, so check the column labels. We compare annual figures in the same currency without converting them to won.
<br/>
Business segments, combined cloud metrics, and revenue from individual AI features cover different activities. Company-wide or cloud revenue cannot all be counted as AI revenue. The two sources didn't establish a comparable standalone AI revenue figure, so we leave it uncalculated. Record what's undisclosed or incomparable rather than filling the gap with an estimate.

## Revenue and Gross Profit

Gross profit is revenue minus cost of revenue. That can include costs for servers and service delivery. Check the company's accounting notes to see how it classifies expenses. Operating income also deducts expenses such as research and development, sales, and administration.

| Company-wide annual figures, USD millions | FY2025 | FY2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue | 281,724 | 331,839 |
| Cost of revenue | 87,831 | 106,374 |
| Gross profit | 193,893 | 225,465 |
| Gross margin, calculated | About 68.8% | About 67.9% |

We checked the figures against the annual income statement in [Microsoft's FY26 Q4 earnings release](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/Investor/earnings/FY-2026-Q4/press-release-webcast).
The margins are the author's calculations of gross profit divided by revenue. They are company-wide figures. Revenue and gross profit both rose in dollar terms while the margin declined. **Read growth and margin separately.** These figures alone don't establish that AI profitability fell.

## Capital Spending and Depreciation

Capital spending acquires assets the company will use to provide services in the future. Depreciation allocates the cost of an asset to earnings over its useful life. The company can pay cash at a different time from when it recognizes the expense.
<br/>
For example, straight-line depreciation on hypothetical equipment costing 600 million won with no residual value is 200 million won a year over three years or 100 million over six. A longer useful life can reduce the expense in a given year. It doesn't recover the upfront cash or guarantee the equipment will stay competitive longer. This is an accounting example, not a Microsoft purchase. The 10-K's accounting policies list the useful lives for its servers and network equipment. [Microsoft FY2026 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526323660/msft-20260630.htm)

Subtracting both depreciation and cash capital spending from operating cash flow can double-count the cost. Operating cash flow already reflects noncash adjustments and changes in working capital. The combined depreciation, amortization, and other line can include more than GPU depreciation. Asset purchases can also involve leases or future payment commitments. Check how reported investment differs from cash paid during the period.

## Calculate Cash Flow After Capital Spending

Operating cash flow measures cash generated and used by operating activities. Here we subtract cash paid for property and equipment. Companies can define adjusted free cash flow differently, so **we use an explicitly defined measure for this comparison**.

| Annual cash measures, USD millions | FY2025 | FY2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operating cash flow | 136,162 | 182,935 |
| Cash additions to property and equipment | 64,551 | 115,948 |
| Simple free cash flow, calculated | 71,611 | 66,987 |

The source figures are the `Net cash from operations` and `Additions to property and equipment` lines in the [FY2026 10-K cash flow statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526323660/msft-20260630.htm).
Operating cash flow rose by 46,773, but cash capital spending rose by 51,397. The amount left after subtraction declined by 4,624. This prompts a closer look at when the investments might pay off and how much cash the business can generate going forward.
<br/>
The remainder differs from cash immediately available for distribution to shareholders. Acquisitions, debt repayments, leases, and dividends create other cash flows. When comparing companies, align the definition of investment and the treatment of leases and stock compensation.

## Contracts and Customer Concentration

Contract backlog differs from recognized revenue and cash collected. For measures tied to future obligations, such as commercial RPO, check when the company expects to recognize revenue and when it bills and collects cash. A large new contract can boost growth while making customer concentration and cash timing more important.
<br/>
Microsoft's 10-K says no single customer accounted for more than 10% of total company revenue. This does not establish how AI contracts are distributed among customers. [Microsoft FY2026 10-K segment notes](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526323660/msft-20260630.htm)
*Total revenue, future contracts, and individual segments use different denominators.* Record the limits of the available data when you can't verify the concentration you want.

## A Worksheet for Reading Disclosures

| What to read | What we checked | Next question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue scope | Separate company-wide and segment figures from AI | Is an AI measure available on the same basis? |
| Costs and margins | Separate increases in dollars from changes in ratios | Which costs are rising as the business grows? |
| Cash investment | Cash additions to property and equipment in the period | How does this differ from leases and commitments? |
| Cash generation | Use the same formula in both years | Does cash remaining after investment recover? |
| Contracts and customers | Check denominators and recognition periods | How much depends on a particular customer or contract? |

Keep figures separate from interpretation and unresolved questions. Semiconductor suppliers and cloud operators have different investments and cost structures, so a single ratio won't rank them. The worksheet helps account for the cash and commitments required to grow.

## Conclusion

When reading AI company results, follow **the connections from revenue to earnings, investment, and cash**. Choose a company and record revenue, cost of revenue, operating cash flow, and cash capital spending for the same period. Leave undisclosed AI figures as questions for a later earnings release.
