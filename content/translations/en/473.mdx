## Before We Begin

I made a comparison table to choose a laptop. I listed weight, screen, battery, and price, but choosing only got harder. I'd left out one question: Would I carry it every day? Collecting more numbers doesn't settle an ETF decision either.
<br/>
**At the top of your comparison, write what you want the fund to do before listing any fund names.** In this final part, we'll bring the questions from the first seven articles into one worksheet and record why we made or deferred a choice.

<Rbox>
The fund cost figures come from prospectuses reviewed on October 20, 2026. This article was prepared on October 21, 2026.
Unverified items are left open. The decision record is neither a recommendation to buy a fund nor a ranking of future performance.
</Rbox>

## Describe the Role in One Sentence

Be more specific than “an ETF with good returns.” You might write “U.S. large-cap exposure for money I'm investing long term.” Add when you'll need the money, in which currency, and how much volatility you can tolerate.
<br/>
For bonds, distinguish money set aside for an upcoming expense from long-term exposure to interest rates. For a distribution fund, decide whether you need cash to spend now or plan to reinvest it. These answers tell you which funds belong in the same comparison.
<br/>
Ranking stocks, bonds, and covered calls by recent gains compares results across investments that do different jobs. **Compare candidates that serve the same role using the same measures.**

## Screen for Must-Have Conditions

Start by defining what each candidate must satisfy before giving it extra points. Here's how to screen candidates for U.S. large-cap exposure.

| Question | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Which index? | Exact index name and PR, TR, or NTR |
| What about exchange rates? | Trading currency and currency hedging policy |
| Does it use another strategy? | Leverage, inverse exposure, call writing, or other structural features |
| Can I buy it in my account? | Eligible funds and markets, and account restrictions |
| Is comparable data available? | Performance using the same period, currency, and distribution treatment |

Whether you can trade a fund in your account and whether it suits you are separate questions. If you can't verify a requirement, record where to check it. [Part 1](https://www.promleeblog.com/blog/post/466-etf-selection-reading-names) covers fund names and codes. [Part 2](https://www.promleeblog.com/blog/post/467-etf-selection-comparing-same-index) covers comparison conditions.

## A Running Comparison of Three Funds

Here is what we found for the TIGER, ACE, and SOL U.S. S&P 500 ETFs. All fee and expense ratios are annual `%` figures.

| Item | TIGER 360750 | ACE 360200 | SOL 433330 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main role | U.S. large-cap stocks | U.S. large-cap stocks | U.S. large-cap stocks |
| Official index label | S&P 500 converted to KRW | S&P 500 PR converted to KRW | S&P 500 PR converted to KRW |
| Hedging policy | Unhedged | Plans to remain unhedged | Plans to remain unhedged |
| Total fees | 0.0068 | 0.0047 | 0.0047 |
| Other expenses | 0.0600 | 0.0660 | 0.0485 |
| Total fees and expenses | 0.0668 | 0.0707 | 0.0532 |
| Costs including underlying funds | Combined total fees and expenses 0.0668 | Definition needs confirmation | Combined total fees and expenses 0.0674 |

The TIGER prospectus has a preparation date of `2026-10-01`, ACE has an effective date of `2026-02-13`, and SOL has a preparation date of `2026-05-22`.
Other expenses reflect past accounting periods specified by the documents. These figures don't capture every cost measured on the same day.
Read securities transaction costs, your brokerage fees, and bid–ask spreads separately from the totals above.
[Part 3](https://www.promleeblog.com/blog/post/468-etf-selection-total-costs) explains what each figure includes and how to convert ratios to won.

## Use the Gaps in the Table to Guide Your Next Checks

Next, record tracking performance and trading conditions. For now, use the same verification notes for all three funds.

| Item | Current record | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Index definition | TIGER's PR/TR details still need checking | Confirm the official index definition |
| Tracking performance | Daily total return data for a common period is missing | Align dividend treatment, currency, and dates for NAV and index |
| Disclosed tracking error | Same definition and period haven't been verified | Record the disclosed formula and observation period |
| Trading conditions | Quotes and quantities at order time haven't been observed | Record the valuation reference and quotes from the same moment |
| Account conditions | Depend on your account | Confirm trading eligibility and fees |

The table leaves us with **three candidates and a decision deferred until the outstanding checks are done**. Missing data tells us what to check. It doesn't give us a zero or tell us the fund is poor.
<br/>
In particular, SOL's 0.0532% total fees and expenses alone doesn't establish that its overall cost is lower. The comparison changes when expenses for underlying funds are included. Cost rankings with mismatched units or periods, or scores based on arbitrary weights, can obscure the reason for your decision.

## Add Questions for Bonds and Income Strategies

Copy the common worksheet and add rows for the role. Comparing every fund on an equity ETF worksheet with only an added distribution yield misses differences in how the strategies work.

| Role | Additional fields |
| --- | --- |
| Bonds | Duration type and date, credit, currency, and maturity or termination terms |
| Dividend stocks | Selection rules, concentration, distribution policy, and total return |
| Covered calls | Underlying assets, share covered by calls, strike prices and contract terms, distribution components, and upside participation |

Longer duration can mean larger price moves when rates change. Receiving a covered-call premium comes with granting the option buyer a right. Consider whether each feature fits the intended role before trying to score it.

## Record Your Decision in Five Lines

Write these five lines under your comparison. Use the same format if you decide to wait.

| Question | Example record |
| --- | --- |
| What role should the fund play? | U.S. large-cap exposure for long-term money |
| What must it meet? | Confirm the exact index, hedging, account conditions, and definition of performance |
| What did I compare? | Official structure and cost items for three funds |
| What's my decision today? | Choose after confirming the index definition, comparable time series, and quotes |
| When will I review it again? | When the missing data becomes available, and when policies, costs, or my goals change |

This is easier to revisit than “everyone else was buying it.” If you've checked everything and only a small difference remains, write down how much it matters at your investment size and time horizon.
The [calculation in Part 3](https://www.promleeblog.com/blog/post/468-etf-selection-total-costs) shows that 0.0068% versus 0.0047% on 10 million won differs by 210 won per year in stated fees. That figure covers the stated fees rather than the difference in total cost.

## Give the Fund a Role in PortRhythm

Comparing three candidates doesn't mean you need to add them all to your holdings. Once you choose one to hold, confirm the code and quantity, then assign it to the relevant asset role. Check your current and target allocations afterward.
<br/>
Switching from fund A to fund B and increasing your allocation to U.S. stocks are separate decisions. After buying another similar fund, check whether your overall equity weight has increased more than you intended.
[Assign assets in PortRhythm](https://www.promleeblog.com/blog/post/439-portrhythm-asset-assignment-guide) · [Check current and target allocations](https://www.promleeblog.com/blog/post/441-portrhythm-current-target-allocation-guide)

<Bbox>
Add one sentence explaining your choice to your monthly review. Next month, start by checking whether the reason still fits what the money is for. If the fund's terms change, compare the affected items again.
</Bbox>

## Conclusion

We started with ETF names and looked at indexes, costs, tracking, and trading conditions. For bond and income strategies, we added questions about how each product works. A completed comparison is **a record of why you chose a fund and what you still need to check**.
<br/>
Pick an ETF you own and record its role, must-have conditions, the information you checked, your decision, and when you would reconsider it. Link the record to your [monthly portfolio report](https://www.promleeblog.com/blog/post/465-portfolio-reading-monthly-review) so your fund selection feeds into your review of the whole portfolio.
