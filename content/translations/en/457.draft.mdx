## Before We Begin

Suppose you read that AI usage and data center investment are rising. A company you follow could still see its stock price fall or its margins come in below expectations. Even if you correctly anticipated the spread of the technology, the investment outcome does not automatically follow. **Industry demand, company profits, and shareholder returns are separate stages.**
<br/>
In this final installment, we turn the costs, pricing, products, infrastructure, and financial results discussed earlier into questions investors can track. The calculations use illustrative scenarios for a hypothetical company. They provide neither price targets nor buy or sell recommendations, and the industry's outlook is kept separate from the author's assumptions. References were checked on October 16, 2026.

## Three Links in the Chain

When usage increases, company revenue may grow at a different rate. A lower processing price or a competitor winning customers changes the relationship between demand and revenue. Even as revenue rises, profits and cash can move in another direction if cost of revenue, operating expenses, and capital spending increase faster. The table in the [previous article on company financial results](/blog/post/456-ai-company-financial-metrics) can be read from the same perspective.
<br/>
Even when a company earns more, earnings per share may rise by a different percentage. Issuing shares to raise capital or compensate employees changes how many shares those earnings are divided among. Finally, if the market already prices in high growth, good results alone do not guarantee a higher valuation. Investors own shares in a specific company at a specific price and under specific conditions.

## Price Competition and Profit

Suppose the document service from our example improves its processing efficiency. If it keeps the savings, margins may improve. If competition forces it to lower prices, customers may capture the savings. Even when lower prices drive usage, support, retrieval, and storage costs still matter. **Whether the technology becomes more efficient and who benefits from that improvement are separate questions.**
<br/>
It also matters how easily customers can switch providers and whether workflow integrations and reliability give them reasons to stay. To judge whether a company has pricing power, check what happens to renewals, churn, usage, and profit per customer. Product complexity or a large volume of data alone does not establish a competitive advantage. The gains may be distributed differently among the company that improves efficiency, its equipment suppliers, its operators, and its customers.

## Recovering Investment and Financing It

Check how much equipment and capital the company needs to grow. Even when the volume of profitable work rises, the business may have to keep spending to replace equipment and expand facilities. When calculating the payback period, factor in replacement investment, operating cash flows, and residual value alongside the initial setup cost. Lower-than-planned utilization or a falling selling price can delay payback.
<br/>
In its data center demand outlook, the IEA treats supply chain bottlenecks, expected returns on investment, and capital market and financing conditions as variables. [IEA, Key Questions on Energy and AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary)
The analysis covers the industry as a whole. Rising financing costs or difficulty refinancing can change what remains for shareholders even when demand is strong. For debt, check the interest and repayment obligations. For an equity raise, check how many shares are added.

## Growth and Valuation Scenarios

Assume the hypothetical company starts with a revenue index of 100, a 20% net margin, and a price-to-earnings ratio of 30 times. The price-to-earnings ratio, or `P/E`, expresses the share price as a multiple of net earnings per share. We normalize the initial share price to 100 and vary the conditions three years later. These illustrations assign no probabilities to the scenarios and make no market forecast.

| Illustrative assumptions after three years | Conservative | Base | Optimistic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Annual revenue growth | 10% | 15% | 20% |
| Final-year net margin | 12% | 18% | 22% |
| Cumulative increase in share count | 10% | 5% | 0% |
| Final-year P/E | 18× | 22× | 26× |
| Revenue index | 133.1 | 152.1 | 172.8 |
| Share price index, calculated | 43.6 | 95.6 | 164.7 |

Here is the calculation for the base scenario. Earnings per share are company-wide earnings divided by the share count, so the calculation accounts for earnings growth and the increase in shares separately.

```text
3년 매출 배수 = 1.15³ = 1.520875
주당 이익 배수 = 1.520875 × (18% ÷ 20%) ÷ 1.05
               ≈ 1.3036
3년 뒤 주가 지수 = 100 × 1.3036 × (22배 ÷ 30배)
                 ≈ 95.6
```

**Revenue grows about 52.1% and earnings per share grow about 30.4%, while the share price index falls about 4.4%.** The figures show *how the share price can fall despite growth*. Higher margins, a steady share count, and a smaller decline in the valuation multiple produce a different outcome. The result measures the price change over three years and excludes dividends, taxes, exchange rates, and trading costs. Annualized returns and the investor's total return would require separate calculations.

## What the Numbers Can Tell Us

The valuation multiple can change as the market reassesses how long growth will last, risks, interest rates, and capital needs. A simple P/E comparison may be unsuitable when one-time investment gains inflate net income or when the company makes a loss. In these cases, assess cash generation and funding needs separately and state the limits of the metric.
<br/>
A more precise scenario table does not make the forecast more accurate. Different combinations of growth, margins, and investment can yield the same result, so inspect the assumptions behind each figure. The SEC's investor education material explains stock prices and the risk of losing your principal. [Investor.gov guide to stocks](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/stocks)
The figures cannot establish the safety of a particular stock or guarantee a return.

<Rbox>
Keep industry growth and share price growth separate, and treat the optimistic case as one possible outcome.

## Investment Tracking Worksheet

Tie the questions you want to check at the next earnings release to specific metrics. Keep expectations and observed figures in separate fields while you gather evidence.

| Hypothesis | Metrics to track | Signal to revise the hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demand translates into revenue | Usage, paid conversion, revenue on a comparable basis | Usage rises while unit prices and conversion fall |
| The company retains the efficiency gains | Gross margin, cost per successful task | Price cuts offset the savings |
| The investment pays back | Utilization, cash flow, cash capital spending | Investment requirements and borrowing keep rising |
| Per-share value also rises | Share count, earnings per share, financing terms | Dilution offsets company-wide earnings growth |

Mark anything the company has not disclosed as unknown. Even when news is better than expected, align definitions and reporting periods before making comparisons. Updating the same table each quarter helps you see where your judgment was off. Tracking the industry, the company, and the stock separately helps distinguish what you understood about the technology from where your investment judgment missed.

## Conclusion

This series started with the cost of running a small AI service and followed it through to company financial results and investment decisions. The same question runs through each installment. **What improved, who benefited, and by how much?** Developers can connect completed tasks to costs and prices. Investors can connect growth to earnings, investment, and per-share metrics. To close out the series, choose your own service or a company you follow and write down your assumptions, observations, and what would change your judgment next time on one page.
