## Before We Begin

I picked the cheapest online listing by looking only at the item price. At checkout, shipping changed which seller was cheapest. An ETF's advertised fee can leave out costs in the same way. **The total fee is an important price tag, but it doesn't show the entire bill.**
<br/>
We'll read the official cost tables for the TIGER, ACE, and SOL U.S. S&P 500 ETFs. The goal is to **explain where each cost is reflected**. We'll also check for double counting, such as adding the same fee twice or subtracting it from returns that already account for it.

<Rbox>
Fund figures come from official materials accessed on October 20, 2026. The examples explain the mechanics of costs. Past ratios do not guarantee future charges or establish a ranking.
</Rbox>

## Costs Arise in Two Places

Separate costs inside the fund from costs you incur when you trade. The fund pays to hold and trade stocks, and you face a different set of costs when buying or selling ETF shares.

<Img src="/etf-selection-03-costs-20261020.webp" width={750} height={500} alt="Fund fees, other expenses, and internal trading costs alongside investor commissions, spreads, and currency conversion costs"/>

| Where the cost arises | What to check | Where to look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inside the fund | Total fees, other expenses, and internal trading costs | Prospectus and fund reports |
| Your trades | Brokerage commissions, bid–ask spreads, and currency conversion costs | Account fee schedule and market quotes |

Fund expenses are paid from assets and affect their remaining value. You don't receive a separate payment notification for them. The fund's expense table also won't tell you everything about your own brokerage charges.
[Investor.gov on fund and ETF costs](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/mutual-fund-and-etf-fees-and-expenses-investor-bulletin)

## Read Past the Total Fees Column

Total fees combine specified charges for management, trustee services, administration, and similar functions. A separate **other expenses** column may include custody, index licensing, and auditing. Read the footnotes to see what the **total fees and expenses** column combines. Avoid adding its components to the total again.
<br/>
Here are the figures from the three prospectuses. All are annual ratios in `%`.

| Item | TIGER 360750 | ACE 360200 | SOL 433330 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Total fees | 0.0068 | 0.0047 | 0.0047 |
| Other expenses | 0.0600 | 0.0660 | 0.0485 |
| Total fees and expenses | 0.0668 | 0.0707 | 0.0532 |

We reviewed page 32 of the TIGER prospectus dated `2026-10-01`, the fees section of the ACE prospectus effective `2026-02-13`, and page 32 of the SOL prospectus dated `2026-05-22`. Other expenses reflect a past accounting period specified in the document, while total fees use the stated rates. **Looking up the figures on the same day doesn't mean they measure actual expenses from the same day.**
[TIGER prospectus](https://investments.miraeasset.com/tigeretf/upload/etf/20261008090949000003.pdf) · [ACE documents](https://papi.aceetf.co.kr/api/funds/K55101D78195/documents) · [SOL official documents](https://www.soletf.com/ko/fund/etf/210930)
<br/>
For example, TIGER's table shows `0.0068 + 0.0600 = 0.0668%`. Adding the 0.0068% fee to 0.0668% counts it twice. Check the labels and footnotes before adding ratios.

## Combined Costs Include a Different Set of Expenses

SOL's prospectus has an additional figure. Total fees and expenses are 0.0532%, while **combined total fees and expenses are 0.0674%**. The combined figure includes fees charged by underlying investment funds, so you can see what those funds cost as well.
<br/>
The difference is `0.0674 − 0.0532 = 0.0142%p`. It corresponds to the prospectus's estimated underlying fund fees of 0.01420%. **The Korean label for this combined cost figure does not identify the fund as a synthetic ETF.** A synthetic structure describes how a fund invests. This line describes which costs are combined.
[SOL prospectus fee section](https://www.soletf.com/ko/fund/etf/210930)
<br/>
Comparing SOL's 0.0532% with another fund's ratio that includes underlying fund fees may compare different sets of expenses. Check what each document includes first.

## Check the Fund's Trading Costs Separately

Securities transaction costs incurred when a fund trades stocks may appear separately from other expenses. ACE lists an annual ratio of 0.0303%, and SOL lists 0.0359% in a separate column. Both figures reflect the historical accounting period `2025-01-01 → 2025-12-31`. TIGER gives the previous period's securities transaction costs as a monetary amount in a separate table.
[ACE expense footnotes](https://papi.aceetf.co.kr/api/funds/K55101D78195/documents) · [SOL expense footnotes](https://www.soletf.com/ko/fund/etf/210930) · [TIGER expense footnotes](https://investments.miraeasset.com/tigeretf/upload/etf/20261008090949000003.pdf)
<br/>
You can't add a percentage and a won amount directly. To convert an amount to a ratio, you need an appropriate denominator, such as average net assets for that period. **Align units and what's included** before filling in costs. Missing data doesn't mean zero.

## Put a Small Percentage in Won

Multiply the rate by your investment to see its scale. Assume the balance stays at 10 million won for a year and only the annual expense rate changes.

| Annual expense rate | Simple one-year equivalent |
| --- | ---: |
| 0.10% | 10,000 won |
| 0.20% | 20,000 won |
| Difference of 0.10 percentage points | 10,000 won |

This illustration excludes monthly balance changes, additional contributions, compounding, and taxes. It doesn't represent a bill for that amount at year-end.
<br/>
On 10 million won, stated total fees of 0.0068% and 0.0047% work out to 680 and 470 won, a difference of 210 won per year. That alone says little about the ranking of total costs. Check other expenses and trading conditions too.

## What You Pay When You Trade

Check the commissions that apply to your own brokerage account. Buying a Korean-listed ETF in won does not automatically incur a personal currency conversion fee just because the fund holds U.S. stocks. Distinguish that from trades that actually require you to convert currency.
<br/>
Quotes affect your costs too. If you can buy at 10,010 won or sell at 9,990 won, each quote is 10 won away from the 10,000-won midpoint. The **bid–ask spread** is `20 ÷ 10,000 = 0.20%`. If prices remain unchanged and enough shares are available, buying 100 shares and immediately selling them would cost 2,000 won in price difference.
<br/>
That's a snapshot of the quotes, and the cost varies with order size, market conditions, and execution prices. If the difference is already reflected in your trading gain or loss, don't deduct the spread again.
[Investor.gov on ETF trading costs](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/mutual-fund-and-etf-fees-and-expenses-investor-bulletin)

## A Final Check for the Cost Worksheet

Record **units, periods, and what's included** alongside the numbers.

- Did you distinguish total fees from total fees and expenses?
- Are underlying fund fees included?
- Are securities transaction costs reported as a ratio or an amount?
- Did you distinguish the accounting period for historical expenses from the current fee rate?
- Did you check your account fees and actual quotes?

<Bbox>
Official NAV returns already reflect fund expenses that have been paid. Deducting the disclosed fee from those returns again can count it twice. Use the expense table to understand why results differ, and separately review any investor costs that aren't included, such as your brokerage commission.
</Bbox>

Use PortRhythm's valuation as a starting point for estimating the amount. Record fees, other expenses, and quotes from official documents and the trading screen. Over a long holding period, recurring costs matter. If you trade often, look at what you pay each time too.

## Conclusion

The stated fee is one part of ETF costs. We separated costs inside the fund from the costs of trading and checked how to avoid adding the same amount twice. **The worksheet shows which costs each figure includes.**
