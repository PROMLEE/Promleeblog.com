## The general structure of a communication system
A communication system generally consists of the following stages, in order.
1. Source: Provides information.
2. Transmitter: Converts information into a transmission signal.
	- Includes a _formatter, source encoder, channel encoder, modulator, and multiplexer_.
3. Channel: Carries the transmitted signal; noise may be present.
4. Receiver: Processes the received signal.
	- Includes a _demultiplexer, demodulator, channel decoder, source decoder, and deformatter_.
5. Destination: The final stage that receives the information.

## Source coding vs. channel coding
- Coding theory: Studies the properties of codes and their suitability for particular applications.
	- Used in data compression, encryption, error correction, and network coding.
	- Generally involves removing redundancy or correcting or detecting errors in transmitted data.
	- Two main aspects:
		- Data compression.
		- Error correction.
- Source encoding: Compresses data at the source for more efficient transmission, as in ZIP and JPEG.
- Channel encoding: Uses additional data bits to detect or correct errors occurring during transmission, as in parity checks, CRC, and Hamming codes.
	- Music CDs: Use Reed–Solomon codes.
	- NASA: Uses turbo codes and low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes.
