## Before We Begin

Suppose you're comparing two friends' race times. If one ran 100 meters and the other ran 200, the times alone won't tell you who was faster. ETFs pose a similar problem. **Even if two funds name the same index, align the way their returns are measured before comparing them.**
<br/>
We'll prepare to compare the TIGER, ACE, and SOL U.S. S&P 500 ETFs. Before asking which return is higher, check **whether the period, currency, and calculation are the same**. This time we'll fill in the conditions at the top of the worksheet.

<Rbox>
Fund information comes from official documents reviewed on October 20, 2026. The example figures illustrate the calculations. They neither rank the funds nor forecast future returns.
</Rbox>

## Read Beyond the Index Name

Once you see S&P 500, check how the index treats dividends. It's like comparing home prices alone versus comparing home prices plus rental income. An index can track price changes or include the effect of reinvesting dividends.

| Label | Plain meaning | Dividend treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PR | Price changes | A price return index that excludes reinvestment of ordinary dividends |
| TR | Price changes plus reinvestment | A total return index that reinvests gross dividends |
| NTR | Return after assumed withholding | A net total return index that adjusts dividends using assumed tax rates |

The NTR tax assumption can differ from the tax you actually pay. Check the index provider's methodology for the calculation and rates.
[S&P DJI index calculation methodology](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/methodologies/methodology-index-math.pdf)
<br/>
ACE's prospectus specifies a price index that excludes dividends. SOL uses `S&P500 Index(PR: Price Return)(원화환산)`, or price return converted to Korean won. TIGER lists `S&P 500 Index(원화환산)`, or S&P 500 converted to won. Alongside the short label, record the official index definition and its treatment of dividends.
[ACE prospectuses](https://papi.aceetf.co.kr/api/funds/K55101D78195/documents) · [SOL official documents](https://www.soletf.com/ko/fund/etf/210930) · [TIGER prospectus](https://investments.miraeasset.com/tigeretf/upload/etf/20261008090949000003.pdf)

## Four Conditions for a Fair Comparison

Record each condition separately.

<Img src="/etf-selection-02-comparison-rules-20261020.webp" width={750} height={500} alt="Four checks to align the period, currency, distributions, and price basis before comparing ETFs"/>

### Period

Use the same start and end dates for every fund. Since-inception returns start at different dates. TIGER and ACE listed on August 7, 2020, and SOL listed on June 21, 2022. The shared comparison period can begin on or after the latest listing date.
[Official listing date: TIGER](https://www.tigeretf.com/ko/product/search/detail/index.do?ksdFund=KR7360750004) · [ACE](https://papi.aceetf.co.kr/api/funds/K55101D78195/product) · [SOL](https://www.soletf.com/ko/fund/etf/210930)
<br/>
If you choose `2025-10-01 → 2026-09-30`, look for data from those dates for all three funds. If data is unavailable on either date, use the same rule for picking a trading day. Keep historical index calculations from before listing separate from the ETF's actual record.

### Currency

This comparison uses Korean won. Comparing a dollar index return directly with a won ETF return mixes in exchange rate effects. Check whether the official benchmark is converted to won and compare currency-hedged products separately. See the [previous series on currency exposure](https://www.promleeblog.com/blog/post/460-portfolio-reading-currency-exposure) for how currencies and prices interact.

### Distributions

Use the same assumption about whether distributions stay in cash or buy more of the same asset. Adding distributions to an official return that already reinvests them counts them twice. Check the footnotes for the reinvestment assumption even when the figures have similar names.

### Price

Net asset value per share, or `NAV`, is the fund's assets minus its liabilities, divided by the number of shares. It can differ from the exchange price at which investors trade.
**Compare NAV with NAV to evaluate fund management, and market price with market price to evaluate trading-price performance.** Mixing the two folds the trading-price gap into the fund's tracking result.
[Investor.gov on ETF prices](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins-24)

## A 5% Price Return and an 8% Total Return

Suppose you buy one share for 100 won, it finishes at 105 won, and you receive a 3-won distribution in between. Assume no additional deposits or withdrawals, reinvestment, taxes, or fees. The price return is `105 ÷ 100 − 1 = 5%`. Including the distribution gives `(105 − 100 + 3) ÷ 100 = 8%`. **The same investment shows 5% or 8% depending on what you count.**
<br/>
Comparing a price-only return for one fund with a return including distributions for another is misleading. This one-share example also assumes no reinvestment. If you buy more shares with each payment, account for the price at each purchase and the larger share count.
<br/>
A PR index and a fund return with reinvested distributions also use different definitions. If a disclosure shows that combination, note that dividend treatment may contribute to the gap. Keep the manager's stated benchmark. If you add a supplementary comparison on a matched basis, record the source and why you added it. This distinction also matters when interpreting tracking results.

## Conditions to Record in Your Worksheet

Before pasting in the numbers, record:

- Comparison start and end dates.
- Currency basis in Korean won and whether the fund is hedged.
- NAV or market price.
- Whether distributions are reinvested.
- The benchmark's PR, TR, or NTR basis and currency conversion method.
- Sources and the date you checked them.

<Bbox>
If you cannot find a definition for any of the three funds, mark it “needs verification.” Knowing what's missing gives you a next step before comparing returns calculated under different conditions.
</Bbox>

In PortRhythm, start by naming the role these three candidates would fill, such as U.S. large-cap exposure. Replacing one product and deciding how much to allocate to that role are separate choices. See [the guide to portfolio composition](https://www.promleeblog.com/blog/post/458-portfolio-reading-consolidated-holdings) for how to interpret those buckets.

## Conclusion

Match the period, currency, distributions, and price basis before comparing returns. **The comparison worksheet records the conditions so you can reproduce the calculation later.**
