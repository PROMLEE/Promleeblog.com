## Principles of congestion control
> Too many sources send data too quickly for the network to handle.

This differs from *flow control*!
**Symptoms**
1. **Packet loss**: When many packets enter the network at once, router buffers can overflow and packets may be lost. This reduces transmission reliability and communication efficiency.
2. **Long delays**: When network traffic exceeds a router's processing capacity, packets must wait in a queue, causing transmission delays.

**Conditions**
- Two senders and two receivers.
- One router with an infinite buffer → No packet loss.
- Output-link capacity: R.
- No retransmissions.

## Causes and costs of congestion: Scenario 2
One router with a *finite* buffer.
The sender retransmits packets that time out.
- Application-layer input equals application-layer output: $\lambda_{in} = \lambda_{out}$.
- Transport-layer traffic includes retransmissions: $\lambda'_{in} \geq \lambda_{in}$.

### Scenario 2-1
An idealized network: The sender has perfect knowledge of the router's buffer state.
- The sender transmits only when the router's buffer has enough space.

### Scenario 2-2
An idealized network: The router reports packet loss.
- The sender retransmits a packet when the router reports it lost.
<Img src="/posts/240615-211131.png" width={250}/>
When transmitting at R/2, some packets are retransmissions, but the asymptotic goodput is still R/2.
This reflects the mechanism that maintains a certain goodput even under congestion.

### Scenario 2-3
A realistic network: Duplicates can occur.
- Packets may be dropped at the router when its buffer fills, so a timeout is needed.
- The sender may time out prematurely, send two copies, and have both delivered.
<Img src="/posts/240615-211131.png" width={250}/>
When transmitting at R/2, some packets are retransmissions, including duplicate packets that are delivered.

#### Costs of congestion
*More work*: Packet loss during congestion forces the sender to retransmit. The network must handle additional work, using resources inefficiently.
*Unnecessary retransmissions*: A premature timeout can make the sender transmit the same packet multiple times, consuming link bandwidth with duplicates.
*Reduced goodput*: More duplicate packets reduce the effective rate of useful delivery, or goodput, lowering overall network performance.

## Scenario 3
**Conditions**
- Four senders and multihop paths.
- Timeouts and retransmissions.
- $\lambda_{in}$: Original data; $\lambda'_{in}$: Retransmitted data.

Q. What happens as $\lambda_{in}$ and $\lambda'_{in}$ increase?
A. As $\lambda'_{in}$ increases, every additional transmitted packet arriving at the upper queue is dropped, and its throughput approaches zero.
#### Another cost of congestion
*Packet loss*: Saturated router buffers can drop packets during congestion, reducing data-transmission reliability and communication efficiency.
