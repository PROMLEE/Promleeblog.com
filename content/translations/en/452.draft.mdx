## Before We Begin

Suppose you launch an unlimited document summarization plan for 20,000 won a month. Most customers use it occasionally, but one processes documents in bulk and pushes up the average cost. If you add limits later, customers may feel that you've changed the deal. **Pricing is a promise about what you provide and what costs extra.**
<br/>
We'll design plans for a hypothetical service. The 20,000 won monthly price includes VAT and is for illustration. As in the previous two articles, assume a cost of 43.64 won per successful task, a 3.3% payment fee, and 91,000 won in monthly base costs. The model includes one development subscription, the server, and the database, and excludes labor, support, and customer acquisition.

## Three Pricing Models

A flat subscription makes the monthly bill predictable, but an unlimited promise leaves you bearing the cost of heavy usage. Usage-based pricing ties revenue to costs, although customers may have trouble forecasting their next bill. A hybrid plan includes an allowance in the base fee and lets users buy more as needed.

| Model | Example price | What customers check | Risk to the operator |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unlimited flat fee | 20,000 won per month | Monthly payment | Cost of heavy users |
| Usage-based | 8,000 won per pack of 100 tasks | Number of packs needed | Low usage and fluctuating revenue |
| Hybrid | 20,000 won per month for 200 tasks, then 8,000 won per extra 100 | Included tasks and cost of extra packs | Managing the allowance and task size |

The usage model assumes prepaid packs of 100 tasks. Check your payment processor's contract for minimum fees and refund costs and add them to the model. The plans include different amounts of work, so the lowest listed price doesn't settle which one is better.

## Included Tasks and Extra Purchases

This example proposes **a hybrid plan with 200 tasks a month**. Define a standard task as a document job with up to 8,000 input tokens and 1,000 output tokens. For a larger document, show the expected number of units before running it and ask the user to approve splitting it, or offer separate pricing. Page count alone is an unreliable limit on model input and cost.
<br/>
Users shouldn't have to count tokens themselves. Show “one standard task” in the interface, explain what document size it covers, and explain how larger jobs convert into task units. Users should be able to connect completed work with the units deducted.

```text
월 포함량 200건을 모두 사용한 고객
공헌이익 = 20,000 ÷ 1.1 − 660 − 200 × 43.64
         ≈ 8,794원

추가 100건 묶음을 모두 사용한 고객
추가 공헌이익 = 8,000 ÷ 1.1 − 264 − 100 × 43.64
              ≈ 2,645원
```

A customer who completes 500 tasks uses the 200 included tasks and buys three extra packs, paying 44,000 won. The contribution margin is 40,000 − 1,452 − 21,820 = 16,728 won. Under the unlimited plan, the same customer would generate a loss of about 4,298 won. *The calculation doesn't tell you whether customers will actually pay 44,000 won.* Test willingness to pay and look at the alternatives available to customers.
<br/>
Higher costs can make extra packs unprofitable too. If every task needs one retry, cost rises to 76.4 won per task even if all eventually succeed. The margin on an extra 100 tasks is then about −631 won. **Review pricing against retry rates and actual task costs as well as usage limits.**

## What Real Subscriptions Can Teach Us

ChatGPT Plus, which includes Codex, and Claude Pro each cost 20 U.S. dollars on monthly billing in the pricing reviewed here. Both describe separate ways to pay for additional usage. Use the distinction between an included allowance and additional purchases as a reference for this example. [OpenAI pricing](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/pricing), [Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)
<br/>
Converting another service's 20-dollar price into won doesn't tell you what to charge. The features, quality, customer needs, and costs of providing the service differ. Pricing was checked on October 15, 2026. The won figures use an assumed rate of 1,400 won per dollar. Using both development tools raises fixed costs to 119,000 won. Don't add those fixed costs to every customer's request cost again.
<br/>
Read the allowance and expiry terms alongside any discount. Claude Max API credits have eligibility terms and expire at the end of the applicable period. They don't carry over like cash. [Claude's guide to included API credits](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/api-credits-for-subscribers)
First assess the example plan using costs before any such benefit.

## Trials and Usage Limits

Assume a free trial with five standard tasks per signup and an overall monthly trial budget of 100,000 won. If every task succeeds at the baseline cost, one trial user costs 218.2 won. A limit on successes won't stop repeated failures, so cap attempts and actual spending separately.
<br/>
Check the monthly budget using both the cost reserved before model calls and cumulative actual usage. When the limit is reached, stop accepting new work or tell users to wait. Account for concurrent requests so they can't collectively exceed the budget. Set limits on document size, concurrent tasks, and retries per account, and add alerts for total spending.

<Bbox>
When a user runs out of included tasks, ask before charging for more. Show the remaining allowance, expected deduction, and payment amount before starting. Run only the work the user approves.
</Bbox>

Disclose when unused units expire and avoid retroactive changes that disadvantage customers. The draft resets the base allowance at monthly renewal and gives extra packs a 30-day window after purchase. These are proposed terms. Before selling, review refunds, cancellation rights, and treatment of paid balances against applicable law and payment policies.

## Failed Tasks and Outcome-Based Pricing

Reserve the estimated units when work starts and deduct them once it meets the completion criteria. In this example, restore reserved units if a service error occurs or the result falls short, and allow at most one automatic retry. Record the `task_id` and billing status so duplicate callbacks or page refreshes don't deduct units twice. **Failed calls still cost the operator money even when the customer isn't charged.** That's why [Part 2's cost per successful task](/blog/post/451-ai-request-total-cost) includes failed calls.
<br/>
A refund and restoration of usage units are separate. Restoring a paid balance doesn't complete a cash refund. Handle customer inquiries and payment cancellations separately. Add the cost of that support and actual refunds before selling, since this draft excludes them.

<Ybox>
Generating a summary and delivering a measurable business outcome are different things. Consider outcome-based pricing when you can agree on the outcome, measurement period, your service's contribution, and a dispute process.
</Ybox>

A document service will have trouble charging for outcomes based solely on an estimate that it saved time. This example starts with measurable standard tasks, then tracks time actually saved and rework separately. Even if a pricing unit covers your costs, check that customers understand and accept it.

## Conclusion

A good plan makes **the scope of service, completion criteria, and extra charges clear**. Write down what is included, how you handle larger tasks and failures, how customers approve extra purchases, and how refunds work. Recalculate whether heavy users still contribute to fixed costs under those terms. Next, we'll distinguish model performance from product competitiveness and look at why customers keep paying.
