> “My stocks are in the brokerage app, my crypto is on an exchange, and my cash is in the bank. How am I supposed to bring all of that together?”

The fields can look daunting at first. What is the ticker? Which number belongs in purchase price? Should dollar cash be entered in won? This guide walks through **adding the assets you own to PortRhythm**. Add stocks and ETFs from a screenshot or manually, search for crypto, and enter cash balances directly. Follow along on desktop or mobile.

<br/>

<Bbox>
The goal is to register your holdings. Check quantity, purchase price, and currency, save, then compare the result with your actual balances. Part 4 explains how to assign assets to investment portfolios.
</Bbox>

[Open PortRhythm →](https://pf.promleeblog.com)

If you have yet to create a target portfolio, start with [Part 2, Setting Up Target Portfolios and Investment Weights](https://www.promleeblog.com/blog/post/437-portrhythm-portfolio-setup-guide). Preparing the portfolios first makes assignment easier.

## Before You Start

The starting menu depends on the asset.

| Asset | Starting menu | Input method | How to save |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Korean stocks and ETFs | Account Management or Import Screenshot | Search and add by account, or import a balance screenshot | Add to This Account or Review and Save |
| U.S. stocks and ETFs | Account Management or Import Screenshot | Search and add by account, or import a balance screenshot | Add to This Account or Review and Save |
| Crypto | Edit Assets | Search the Bithumb won market, then enter quantity and purchase price | Add → Save Crypto and Cash Changes |
| Won or dollar cash and other assets tracked by amount | Edit Assets | Enter name, amount, and currency | Add → Save Crypto and Cash Changes |

**Enter share quantity for stocks and ETFs and the balance amount for cash.** If you hold ten shares worth KRW 2 million, enter ten in the share field. If you have KRW 2 million in cash, enter that amount in the cash field.

<br/>

#### Information to Have Ready

- **Stocks and ETFs.** Brokerage and account label, Korean or U.S. market, security name and ticker, current quantity, and average purchase price per share.
- **Crypto.** Exact coin name, current quantity, and average purchase price per coin.
- **Cash.** Balance, won or dollar currency, and the portfolio you want to use.

The screenshots use a local test account. Ten Apple shares, 0.05 Ethereum, and USD 1,000 in cash are **examples for learning the inputs**. Screen prices are snapshots from the time of capture. The entered prices are illustrative.

<br/>

## 1. Importing a Screenshot

#### 1.1 Desktop

Choose **Tools → Import Screenshot** in the left menu. Import Assets From a Screenshot has three areas.

- **01 Select Screen.** Identify the brokerage, account, and market.
- **02 Upload Screenshot.** Read securities and numbers from the balance image.
- **03 Review Details.** Edit recognized data or add rows manually, then save.

<Img src="/portrhythm-assets-desktop-import-20261008.webp" width={800} alt="Desktop screenshot import showing screen selection, screenshot upload, and review sections"/>

<br/>

#### 1.2 Mobile

Tap **All → Import Screenshot** in the bottom menu. The desktop panels stack vertically on mobile. Fill in the screen selection, then scroll down to choose a file and review the results.

<Img src="/portrhythm-assets-mobile-import-20261008.webp" width={350} alt="Mobile screenshot import with brokerage, account label, and Korean or overseas holdings selection"/>

The form is long on mobile. Dismiss the keyboard and scroll if it covers the next field.

<br/>

## 2. Brokerage and Account Labels

#### 2.1 The Brokerage Identifies the Source

Enter the brokerage that holds the balance. Check any prefilled name against your actual brokerage. The account label distinguishes accounts at the same firm. Use a recognizable label such as **Regular Account, ISA, or Pension Savings**. An account number is unnecessary.

| Field | Example | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brokerage | Meritz | Matches the brokerage holding the assets |
| Account label | Regular Account | Distinguishes it from other accounts |
| Balance screen | Korean Stocks or Overseas Stocks | Matches the securities' market |

<br/>

#### 2.2 Choosing Korean or Overseas Stocks

Select **Korean Stocks** for stocks and ETFs listed in Korea, with prices in won. Select **Overseas Stocks** for U.S.-listed stocks and ETFs, with prices in dollars. The overseas option on this screen is designed for U.S. securities.

A Korean-listed U.S. index ETF belongs under **Korean Stocks** even if its name mentions the United States. Check **where the security is listed**.

<Ybox>
Check the market before adding securities. Changing the balance screen after adding rows leaves those rows' markets as they were. Remove any incorrect row and add it again under the correct market.
</Ybox>

<br/>

#### 2.3 Reuse the Same Account Label

If you use Regular Account today, use it again when updating that balance. The app may treat Primary Account or General Account as a different account. **Keep each account's label consistent** to prevent duplicates, and distinguish genuinely separate accounts.

<br/>

## 3. Balance Screenshots

#### 3.1 Preparing a Readable Screenshot

Capture the brokerage screen that shows securities and quantities. If possible, include names, share counts, prices, and purchase information in one view. Tiny text or heavy cropping makes recognition harder. If you take multiple screenshots of a long list, check for overlapping securities.

<br/>

Click **Choose File** in Upload Screenshot. Pick the saved image on desktop or a screenshot from Photos or Files on mobile. Use an image such as PNG or JPG.

The browser reads the text after you choose the image. The first run may take longer while it loads recognition resources. When recognition finishes, scroll to **Review Details**.

<Bbox>
The browser analyzes the screenshot locally. The original image stays off the server. Review and Save stores the reviewed securities, quantities, and purchase information in the signed-in account.
</Bbox>

<br/>

#### 3.3 Recognition Produces a Draft

Recognition can misread similar characters or small numbers. Check for truncated ETF names, misplaced decimal points, or a number mistaken for a price. **Review and save the results after choosing the file** to complete registration.

<br/>

## 4. Checking Names and Tickers

On desktop, fields appear side by side in each row. On mobile, they stack. These screenshots show **an example created with Add Manually**. Review OCR results in the same area.

<Img src="/portrhythm-assets-desktop-review-20261008.webp" width={800} alt="Desktop review showing manually added Apple, ticker AAPL, quantity 10, screenshot price 180, and purchase price 170"/>
<Img src="/portrhythm-assets-mobile-review-20261008.webp" width={350} alt="Mobile review with name, ticker, quantity, screenshot price, purchase price, and Review and Save"/>

<br/>

#### 4.1 Is the Security Name Enough?

The name is for people to read. The **ticker** identifies the security for price lookup.

| Type | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Korean security | 005930 | Identifier for a Korean listing |
| U.S. security | AAPL | U.S. ticker |
| U.S. exchange | NAS, NYS, AMS, and others | Exchange identifier used for quote lookup |

Verify **ticker, market, and exchange** when ETFs have similar names or securities trade in different markets. Korean tickers can start with zero, and some newer codes can contain letters. Preserve leading zeros.

<br/>

#### 4.2 Finding a Code With KIS

- **Enter the security name or a code you know.** Correct a name truncated by the screenshot first.
- **Click Find KIS Code.** This looks up candidates for quote retrieval.
- **Choose the matching security.** If several results appear, check ticker and exchange. An unambiguous match may be applied immediately.
- **Verify the resulting code.** Compare it with your brokerage statement before entering numbers.

A ticker is required for U.S. securities. Verify Korean codes too so the app can retrieve prices.

<Ybox>
Run Find KIS Code again after changing the name or ticker. Changing the name clears ticker and exchange verification. Changing the ticker clears exchange verification. Check again using the final entries.
</Ybox>

## 5. Entering Quantity and Price

Distinguish **total amounts from prices per unit**.

| Field | What to enter | Common mix-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quantity | Shares currently held | Valuation or just the latest purchase quantity |
| Screenshot price | Price per share when the image was captured | Total position value |
| Purchase price | Average purchase price per share | Total purchase cost or current price |

**Use won for Korean listings and dollars for U.S. listings.** Enter the U.S. purchase price in dollars directly.

<br/>

#### 5.1 Entering Ten Apple Shares

Suppose you own ten Apple shares with an average purchase price of USD 170 and a screenshot price of USD 180.

| Field | Example value |
| --- | ---: |
| Security name | Apple |
| Ticker | AAPL |
| Quantity | 10 |
| Screenshot price | 180 |
| Purchase price | 170 |

Total purchase cost is ten times USD 170, or USD 1,700. **Enter 170 as the purchase price.** The screenshot price gives a valuation of USD 1,800. A fresher quote after saving can change it.

<Gbox>
Enter the price per unit and the number of units. The app multiplies them to calculate the amount.
</Gbox>

<br/>

#### 5.2 If You Do Not Know the Purchase Price

Use the brokerage's average purchase price if available. Otherwise, leave the field blank. A price and quantity are sufficient for valuation, while purchase-based gains and losses also need a purchase price. **Keep unknown purchase prices blank** instead of entering zero or a current quote.

<Toggle title="What if I own fractional shares?">
Enter the decimal quantity shown by your brokerage. Stock quantity edits currently allow up to six decimal places. A misplaced decimal changes the valuation substantially, so check that you entered 0.5 shares instead of 5.
</Toggle>

<br/>

## 6. Adding Stocks Without a Screenshot

To add a stock or ETF directly, you can start in **Account Management**. Open it from the desktop sidebar or `전체 → 자산 관리 → 계좌 관리` (All → Asset Management → Account Management) on mobile. Create the brokerage and account nickname, then use `종목 직접 추가` (Add Holding Manually) to choose the Korean or U.S. market and search. Select the result and enter current total shares and the average purchase price per share. Verify quantity and price in `총매입금액 검산` (Total Purchase Cost Check), then click `이 계좌에 추가` (Add to This Account). [Part 10, Brokerage Account Management](https://www.promleeblog.com/blog/post/445-portrhythm-brokerage-account-guide) covers adding, editing, and moving positions by account.

<br/>

Here is how to use **Add Manually inside Import Screenshot**. Its save control differs from Account Management, so check which screen you are on.

Manual entry can be easier if your brokerage is unsupported or you have only a few securities. It works without an image.

- **Fill in Select Screen first.** Choose the brokerage, account label, and Korean or overseas market.
- **Click Add Manually under Review Details.** A new row appears.
- **Verify the name and ticker** with Find KIS Code.
- **Enter the numbers.** Fill in quantity and any known screenshot price and purchase price.
- **Click Add Manually again as needed.** Add securities from the same brokerage, account, and market together.

Use the **x** on the right to remove unwanted rows from the unsaved list. For a different brokerage or account, save the current batch, change the selection, and enter the other balance separately.

<br/>

## 7. Reviewing and Saving

Check three things before saving.

- **Source.** Are the brokerage, account label, and Korean or U.S. market correct?
- **Security.** Do the name, ticker, and exchange match your actual holding?
- **Numbers.** Are the quantity, price per share, average purchase price per share, and currency correct?

Click **Review and Save** when ready. After success, the app opens Holdings. Check that the security appears and its quantity matches your balance.

<br/>

#### 7.1 Entering the Same Balance Again

Entering the same ticker under the same brokerage, account, and market updates the existing balance. Check that the exchange matches too. If you started with ten shares and bought two, enter **the current total of twelve shares**. The saved position becomes twelve.

| Situation | Quantity to enter | Saved quantity |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Add ten shares initially | 10 | 10 |
| Buy two more and update the same account | 12 | 12 |
| Later sell five and update the same account | 7 | 7 |

<Ybox>
A different account label can create a separate balance. Keep the label consistent for the same account. Entries without tickers may also remain separate even when their names match.
</Ybox>

Screenshot import updates the holdings you enter. It leaves existing holdings that are missing from the new screenshot in place. Remove fully sold positions separately and reconcile the list against your actual balance.

## 8. Searching for Crypto

After stocks, add crypto under **Edit Assets** in the desktop sidebar or **All → Edit Assets** on mobile. Scroll past stocks to **Edit Crypto and Cash** and expand it if collapsed.

<br/>

#### 8.1 Choosing the Right Coin

Enter a name or code under Crypto · Bithumb Won Prices. Search for **Ethereum** or **KRW-ETH**, for example, then select the coin you actually hold. Ethereum, Ethereum Classic, and Ethereum Name Service are distinct. Check the **market code** as well as the name. Verify the selected label, such as Ethereum · KRW-ETH, before entering quantity.

<Img src="/portrhythm-assets-desktop-crypto-20261008.webp" width={800} alt="Desktop crypto entry selecting Ethereum KRW-ETH with quantity 0.05 and purchase price per coin of 3000000 won"/>
<Img src="/portrhythm-assets-mobile-crypto-20261008.webp" width={350} alt="Mobile crypto form with Ethereum search, quantity, purchase price per coin, portfolio, and Add"/>

<br/>

#### 8.2 Bithumb Won Prices

The app **values holdings using Bithumb's won market**. Enter your balance yourself. Coins held elsewhere can use the Bithumb price if the same won market exists, though your exchange may show a different valuation. Use only the exact coin you hold when selecting a market.

<br/>

## 9. Entering Coin Quantity and Purchase Price

#### 9.1 Enter the Exact Quantity

If you hold 0.05 Ethereum, enter **0.05**. Use quantity here and enter a positive number with up to twelve decimal places. In Purchase Price per Coin (KRW), enter **the average price of one coin**.

| Item | Example input | Meaning |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Coin | Ethereum · KRW-ETH | Market used for valuation |
| Quantity | 0.05 | Ethereum held |
| Purchase price per coin | 3,000,000 | Average price of one Ethereum in won |
| Total purchase cost | KRW 150,000 | 0.05 × 3,000,000 |

Entering the KRW 150,000 total in the price field would change the calculation. **Enter 3,000,000 in this example.**

<br/>

For 0.01 BTC bought at an average of KRW 80 million per BTC, enter quantity 0.01 and price 80,000,000. Total purchase cost is KRW 800,000. You can leave purchase price blank if unknown. As with stocks, the app may be able to value the holding while leaving purchase-based gains or losses unknown.

<br/>

#### 9.2 Choose a Portfolio, Then Add

Choose a portfolio next to the quantity and purchase price. Check the default against your plan. The test account's Bitcoin portfolio is only an example. Choose the one that fits your Ethereum holding. Review the entries, click **Add**, and confirm the coin appears below.

<Bbox>
Add puts crypto on the pending list. Click Save Crypto and Cash Changes to save it to your account. On mobile you can also use Save Changes at the bottom.
</Bbox>

<br/>

#### 9.3 If You Bought More of an Existing Coin

Each market can have one entry. If Bitcoin already exists, **edit its quantity and purchase price per coin**. If tracking the same coin across exchanges or wallets, enter the total for that market. The app tracks one balance per crypto market.

<Toggle title="How do I average the purchase price across several trades?">
Weight each purchase price by its quantity. If you bought 0.01 BTC at KRW 80 million per BTC and 0.02 BTC at KRW 90 million, you have 0.03 BTC at a total purchase cost of KRW 800,000 plus KRW 1.8 million, or KRW 2.6 million. **KRW 2.6 million divided by 0.03** gives about KRW 86,666,667 per BTC. If your exchange provides an average, check how it treats fees and use that figure.
</Toggle>

<br/>

## 10. Adding Won, Dollar, and Other Manual Balances

Scroll down in Edit Assets to **Other Manually Entered Assets**. Enter the amount directly for cash or any asset whose valuation you track separately.

<Img src="/portrhythm-assets-mobile-cash-20261008.webp" width={350} alt="Mobile manual asset form with name, amount, won or dollar currency, portfolio, and Add"/>

<br/>

#### 10.1 Adding KRW 500,000

- **Name.** Use a recognizable label such as Spending Account Cash or Cash Awaiting Investment.
- **Currency.** Select won.
- **Amount.** Enter **500000** for KRW 500,000.
- **Portfolio.** Choose a portfolio or leave it unclassified.
- **Add.** Confirm that the name and balance appear below.

Check that you entered the intended amount before adding.

<br/>

#### 10.2 Adding USD 1,000

Select **dollars** and enter **1000**. The app handles conversion.

| Cash held | Currency to select | Amount to enter |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| KRW 500,000 | Won | 500000 |
| USD 1,000 | Dollars | 1000 |

At an illustrative rate of KRW 1,350 per dollar, USD 1,000 is valued at KRW 1.35 million. The app uses its configured rate.

<Ybox>
If you select dollars and enter 1,350,000, the app reads USD 1.35 million. Enter the dollar balance and let the app convert it.
</Ybox>

The screenshot uses the test account's exchange rate at the time. If your converted amount differs, check [the exchange rate settings in Part 2](https://www.promleeblog.com/blog/post/437-portrhythm-portfolio-setup-guide).

<br/>

#### 10.3 Which Cash Should You Include?

Decide which assets you want to compare. Including both investment cash and a spending account changes total value and weights. Creating a **portfolio** called Cash Awaiting Investment and adding a cash **asset** are separate actions. The portfolio name alone carries no value.

Manual balances are entered by you. After deposits, withdrawals, or other changes, edit and save the amount. Dollar assets retain their entered balance in dollars and use the configured rate for the won valuation.

<Toggle title="Can I enter the same holding as a security and a manual asset?">
Adding ten shares and also entering their value as a manual asset counts the same holding twice. The same applies to crypto. Use manual entries for balances you track separately. Entries with matching names do not automatically merge, so check for duplicate balances in the list.
</Toggle>

## 11. Saving Crypto and Cash Changes

Once crypto and cash are on the list, save the changes. Click **Save Crypto and Cash Changes** below the section on desktop. On mobile, use the same button or **Save Changes** at the bottom.

<Img src="/portrhythm-assets-mobile-save-20261008.webp" width={350} alt="Mobile save controls after adding dollar cash to the pending list"/>

*The screenshot shows where to save. The test entry was left unsaved.*

After the **Saved** indicator appears, open Holdings and check the registered crypto and cash. The app fetches crypto prices and values each holding based on quantity.

<br/>

An individual stock row has its own Save control. It leaves pending cash edits for the separate crypto and cash save. Verify saving in each area you changed.

<Gbox>
For stocks, use Review and Save. For crypto and cash, add them to the list and save the changes.
</Gbox>

## 12. Final Checks in Holdings

Compare actual balances with PortRhythm. Start with **quantities and currencies by asset** before looking at the total.

| Item | What to verify |
| --- | --- |
| Stock account labels | The same account appears under one consistent label |
| Stock purchase price | Average per share |
| Crypto | Exact market code and total quantity |
| Crypto purchase price | Average won price per coin |
| Cash | Correct name, balance, and won or dollar currency |
| Manual entries | No duplicate balances or already registered holdings |
| Saving | All pending additions were saved |

<br/>

#### 12.1 When a Brokerage Shows a Different Valuation

Even with matching quantities, valuations can differ because of **quote timing and exchange rates**. For stocks, the app uses a fetched price when available and may fall back to a screenshot price. Check the source and timestamp. Crypto uses Bithumb won prices, which can differ from other exchanges. Dollar assets are converted at PortRhythm's configured rate. Verify quantities, unit prices, and currencies first, then compare price sources and rates.

## Troubleshooting

<Toggle title="Why is the security list empty after I uploaded a screenshot?">
Check that the brokerage and balance format are supported and that the image clearly shows securities and quantities. Use Add Manually if recognition is unsuitable. You can register holdings without OCR.
</Toggle>

<Toggle title="Why does saving ask me to check the ticker or quantity?">
U.S. securities require a ticker. Check the name and market, then run Find KIS Code. Quantity must be greater than zero. Optional prices must be positive when entered. Leave unknown values blank and verify that brokerage and account labels are filled in.
</Toggle>

<Toggle title="Why is a stock price missing or incorrect?">
Verify ticker, market, and exchange. For an incorrect code, use the code verification or change control in Edit Assets. If the app cannot fetch a quote and no screenshot price is available, it may lack a price for valuation. Verify the actual security before choosing a replacement code.
</Toggle>

<Toggle title="Why can't I add the coin I searched for?">
Select the coin from the results. Quantity must be positive, as must purchase price if entered. If the same market already exists, edit its quantity and purchase price. If the search returns nothing, check whether Bithumb has a supported won market for the coin.
</Toggle>

<Toggle title="Why did my cash disappear after refreshing?">
Add stages the entry. Click Save Crypto and Cash Changes or the mobile Save Changes button, then confirm completion before navigating. Also verify that you signed in with the original provider and account.
</Toggle>

<Toggle title="Why is the amount hundreds or thousands of times higher than expected?">
Check the units first. A won-converted price entered for a U.S. share or a won amount entered for dollar cash can inflate the result. Enter the price per share or coin, the actual quantity held, and the actual dollar balance in their respective fields. Check for duplicates too.
</Toggle>
