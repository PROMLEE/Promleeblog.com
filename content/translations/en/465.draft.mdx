## Before We Begin

I checked my accounts yesterday, but I can't quite remember why I bought that ETF last month. I have screenshots and plenty of numbers, but no sentence recording what I was thinking. What helps is **one page with short answers to the same questions**.
<br/>
This is the final installment of Reading Your Portfolio. We'll bring the previous seven worksheets together into a monthly review. You don't need to buy something every month. **Use the review to record why you decided against trading, too.**

<Rbox>
The balances, transactions, and review dates are examples of how to keep a monthly record. They do not represent real investments or recommendations.
</Rbox>

## Use the Same Measures Each Month

If you use a different ruler every time you measure your height, it's hard to tell how much you've grown. Use the same scope, currency, and valuation times when comparing your assets from month to month. Here we combine only the investments in General A, General B, and Long-Term C, leaving out living expenses and emergency savings.
<br/>
Beyond marking a snapshot as month-end, record **when you checked prices and exchange rates**. Note holdings with unconfirmed prices or balances entered late so an asset added this month doesn't look like an investment gain. The starting balances appear below.

## A Month in Which 10 Million Won Became 11 Million

Set the start to `2026-09-01 09:00` and the end to `2026-10-01 09:00`, using Korea time and won. **Assume every asset price stays the same and distributions, costs, and taxes are zero.**
<br/>
These are the only events this month. Trades are shown at their transaction amounts, and transfers between accounts stay within the portfolio.

| Time | Trade or cash flow | Money added to the portfolio |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| September 15, 10:00 | Sell 400,000 won of bond ETF D in C | 0 won |
| Same day, 10:10 | Transfer 400,000 won of cash from C to A | 0 won |
| Same day, 10:20 | Buy 600,000 won of ETF A in A | 0 won |
| October 1, 08:30 | Deposit 1 million won into C from outside the portfolio | +1 million won |
| Same day, 08:40 | Buy 600,000 won of bond ETF D in C | 0 won |
| Same day, 08:45 | Buy 50,000 won of gold ETF E in C | 0 won |

The first equity purchase used 200,000 won already in A and the 400,000 won transferred in. Of the final 1 million won deposit, 600,000 won went to bonds and 50,000 won to gold, leaving 350,000 won in cash.

| Account and asset | Starting value | Current value |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| A · U.S. equity ETF A | 3,000,000 won | 3,600,000 won |
| A · Individual stock X | 500,000 won | 500,000 won |
| A · KRW cash | 500,000 won | 300,000 won |
| B · U.S. growth ETF B | 1,500,000 won | 1,500,000 won |
| B · Korean equity ETF C | 1,000,000 won | 1,000,000 won |
| C · KRW bond ETF D | 2,000,000 won | 2,200,000 won |
| C · Gold ETF E | 500,000 won | 550,000 won |
| C · KRW cash | 1,000,000 won | 1,350,000 won |
| Total | **10 million won** | **11 million won** |

The account totals are 4.4 million won in A, 2.5 million in B, and 4.1 million in C. Equities increased by 600,000 won because **we bought more with money already in the portfolio**. The 1 million won increase in the total came from the external deposit. The investment gain is zero, as shown by `1,100만 − 1,000만 − 100만 = 0원` (11 million minus 10 million minus 1 million won).

## What to Bring Forward from Earlier Worksheets

Add what you learned beyond the balances. Separate **figures to recalculate from this month's balances** from **assumptions to keep in your notes**.

### Composition and Target Weights

The portfolio holds 6.6 million won in stocks, 2.2 million in bonds, 550,000 in gold, and 1.65 million in cash. The current weights are 60%, 20%, 5%, and 15%, matching the targets. After checking the recorded transactions, you can note that no additional trade is needed to adjust the weights this month. Separately confirm that the targets still fit your time horizon and finances.

### Overlapping Companies and Currency

Stock X makes up 6% of ETF A and 10% of ETF B. The other holdings contain no X shares. After buying more ETF A, combined exposure to X is `360만 × 6% + 150만 × 10% + 50만 = 86.6만 원`, or 866,000 won. Dividing by 11 million won gives about 7.87%.
<br/>
The amount invested in X rose from 830,000 won, but the weight fell from 8.3% because **the contribution increased the whole portfolio**. Record both the amount and weight to explain the change.
<br/>
Unhedged U.S. equity exposure through A, B, and X is 5.6 million won, about 50.91% of the total. Of that, the dollar-traded A and X account for 4.1 million won, or about 37.27%. B trades in won but still has currency exposure. Gold ETF E is 5%, and its structure remains unconfirmed, so record *check currency hedge*. This review considers direct currency exposure. Indirect effects such as a company's imports and exports are separate.

### Performance and a Downside Scenario

Because prices never changed, the time-weighted return (`TWR`), which separates cash flows, is 0%, and `XIRR`, which accounts for deposit dates, is 0% annualized. **The 1 million won contribution did not count as a return.**
<br/>
Apply −30% to equities, −5% to bonds, +10% to gold, and 0% to cash. The 11 million won portfolio would lose 2.035 million won. Even if the weights stay the same, a larger portfolio can lose more money in absolute terms. Revisit living expenses, emergency savings, and upcoming payments.

## A One-Page Monthly Review

Copy this worksheet and replace the figures and notes with your own. **Write it as a handoff to yourself next month.**

| Question | This month's record |
| --- | --- |
| What money do I manage? | Long-term investments across three accounts, excluding living expenses and emergency savings |
| What dates am I comparing? | 2026-09-01 09:00 → 2026-10-01 09:00, Korea time and KRW |
| What happened to the total and cash flows? | 10 million → 11 million won, with a net external contribution of 1 million won |
| What did the investments earn? | 0 won profit or loss, 0% TWR and 0% annualized XIRR, assuming unchanged prices |
| How is it allocated? | 60% stocks, 20% bonds, 5% gold, and 15% cash, matching the target |
| Where do companies overlap? | X is 866,000 won or about 7.87%, using 6% in A and 10% in B |
| What is the currency exposure? | About 50.91% unhedged U.S. equities. Gold ETF E's structure needs checking |
| What if markets fall? | −2.035 million won. Compare with money you need |
| What is the cash for? | Divide 1.65 million won into 800,000 for the next investment, 500,000 for settlement, and 350,000 for adjustments |
| What's this month's decision and why? | No additional trades because weights match targets. Read the gold ETF prospectus |
| When is the next review? | 2026-11-01, or earlier if finances change |

When you don't know a value, record **what is unconfirmed and which source to check**.

<Bbox>
You don't need to fill every field at once. Start with the total, external cash flows, and the purpose of cash. Add company overlap or currency exposure at the next review. Knowing what is missing helps you decide what to do next.
</Bbox>

## Walk Through PortRhythm

Follow the same order in the app.

- **Accounts and holdings**  
  Check for missing accounts, incorrect quantities, duplicate cash, and unconfirmed prices.
- **Asset assignment**  
  Look for assets in the wrong bucket or without a category.
- **Portfolio weights**  
  Compare current and target allocations over the same scope.
- **Asset history**  
  Compare valuation changes and external cash flows over the same period.

For the controls, see [account management](https://www.promleeblog.com/blog/post/445-portrhythm-brokerage-account-guide), [asset assignment](https://www.promleeblog.com/blog/post/439-portrhythm-asset-assignment-guide), [allocation comparison](https://www.promleeblog.com/blog/post/441-portrhythm-current-target-allocation-guide), and the [asset history guide](https://www.promleeblog.com/blog/post/443-portrhythm-asset-history-guide). Add your calculations and the reasons for your decisions to complete the monthly report.
<br/>
If the numbers match your plan but new headlines make you want to trade, write one sentence explaining why. “The date I need the money has changed” and “today's stock market scared me” describe different changes. **A record lets you revisit your reasoning later.**

## Conclusion

We began by bringing the accounts together. Now one page can explain where the money is, which companies and currencies it is exposed to, why the balance changed, and what still needs checking. **The point of a monthly review is to ask the same questions on the same basis.**
<br/>
Open [PortRhythm](https://pf.promleeblog.com/) and record this month's total and external cash flows. Then add a sentence about what you did and why. If you didn't trade, record that reason too.
